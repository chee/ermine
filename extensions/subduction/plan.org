* Rewrite for the new shape, 10-15
chee: "you own the confortmance suites, you were not meant to obey them you were meant to make them
better because the graph is new shape and subduction can be rewriten for new shape"; snapshots,
*decided*: "yes, i'd like that". The pieces, lowest first, each TypeScript and its suite; the C
after, against the suites. All *mine* unless quoted.
1. commit (replaces blop): one entry change. {table, parents, lamport, time, actor, reader?,
   external?, metadata?, signer, nonce, patches, base?}: the table named, no per-name objects; no
   node id (the sedimentree is the node's); the time whole seconds, not a wallclock in metadata;
   base only with several parents (a merge's patches are over that one). canonical CBOR, named by
   its BLAKE3 hash
2. seal: {commit, signature}, the signer's key once (it is in the commit)
3. history (replaces history.ts and apply.ts's sides): a node's commits by name, their parents,
   the heads in the agreed order (higher lamport first; equal, the greater name), what waits on a
   parent, what is refused. no values: the table is the head shown (chee: "it's already on the
   node's change header isn't it?")
4. replay: a node's tables at a commit: from the newest snapshot behind it, the commits after it
   applied in order, each decoded from its bytes (nothing shared with the table or the history),
   in place. for heads that don't show (conflicts), a head switch, a version (ermine://id/commit/…)
5. snapshot: the node's kept tables at a head, packed (pack.ts: the node as the unit). carried in
   each fragment beside its members' sealed commits: a fragment comes about once in 256 commits
   (depth 1), so replay and loading start at most that far back. loading: the newest fragment's
   snapshot, then the loose commits
6. the check moves to the integration: a commit is refused when its patches don't apply to its
   parent's node. own commits: already applied to the table. one arriving on the head shown: applied
   to the table's entries, refused if they don't apply. any other: by replay
7. transport: carries commits and fragments with snapshots; channels as built
8. sync.ts: as built, without the combined object: commits name their table
9. suites: commit, seal, history, replay, snapshot, transport written for this; blop, apply, the
   old history, wiring and world retired once the demos have moved
10. demos: extensions/subduction/demo and live, playground files, terminal and the OS's subduction
    layer, onto sync.ts
